# Dunya story proposal — Displaced: Faith Beyond Borders
Status: specification only, not implemented. Mature subject matter, trauma-sensitive opt-in, legal and religious review required.

## Premise
An eligible fictional noncitizen protagonist in the United States encounters immigration enforcement, detention, legal review and a possible third-country removal. Destination selection is **conditional**, not an unrestricted random Muslim-country wheel. A country being Muslim-majority is not itself a lawful removal basis. If a reviewed fictional branch ends in removal, the next chapter loads the corresponding city pack with persistent relationships, skills and personal inventory as appropriate; it is a new chapter, not punishment or a forced reset of faith progress. Alternative branches include release, legal challenge, case continuation, and family/community support. Do not assume Ahmed or Fatimah has an immigration status.

- 2026 lawmakers' inquiry about reported religious-accommodation failures: https://pou.house.gov/media/press-releases/pou-leads-27-colleagues-calling-dhs-protect-religious-rights-ice-detention
Policies and litigation change; require dated legal review per release. Official standards describe obligations, not proof of actual facility compliance. Facility-specific conditions must be verified and cannot be generalized.

## Branching story chapters
1. Intake and dignity: language access, understanding documents, contacting counsel and family, healthcare and property records; no evasion advice.
2. Faith in detention: ask for Qur'an, prayer space, prayer schedule, wudu/water access, halal or otherwise suitable religious diet, Ramadan meal timing, modest clothing/hijab, chaplain, congregational prayer when permitted; restrictions and accommodation request process depend on facility standards.
3. Disruption and accommodations: respond to delayed meal, missing text, limited space or denied request with safe grievance, counsel and documented review. No automatic claim that every facility violates policy.
4. Solidarity: kindness toward detainees of all religions, multilingual assistance, reading, reflection, mutual aid, health and mental well-being. No recruiting, gang hierarchy, harassment or violence.
5. Legal process: notices, interpreter, lawyer, hearings, protection claims, contested third-country procedures and possible appeal. The game is not legal advice. Never teach falsification, concealment or evasion.
6. Outcome: release, case pending, transfer, or legally contextualized removal. Country selection restricted to a reviewed scenario manifest containing a lawful narrative basis and risk checks.
7. Arrival and rebuilding: interpreter, safe accommodation, local mosque/community services, legal status support, work, language and ethical commerce; preserve player agency and family ties.

## Location and institution modeling
Distinguish ICE civil detention, local jail holding ICE detainees, federal prison and state prison; do not interchange standards. For each facility archetype: jurisdiction, governing standards, contractual type, year, religious accommodation rules, grievance procedure, legal access, visitation, health, accessibility, food service and review provenance. No real facility modeled without verification and sensitivity review.

## Religious education
Qur'an 2:286 (capacity), 94:5–6 (hardship and ease), 2:153 (patience/prayer), 4:135 (justice) are research leads; qualified reviewers must check context, translations and applicability. Teach prayer under constraints and tayammum only with fiqh-specific scholarly review, not universal automatic rulings. Voluntary learning and respectful depiction; never imply detention is divine punishment.

## Randomization and safety
Randomize events only within approved legal/facility/city scenario sets. No arbitrary country selection, no stereotyping of Muslim-majority nations, no profiling immigration status by appearance or religion. Player can skip detention scenes and access a non-graphic summary; content warnings, no humiliating mechanics, no reward for suffering, no violence or escape simulation. Sensitive real-world experiences should be reviewed with affected communities and legal professionals.

## Acceptance
- Every factual legal claim linked to dated official or credible legal source and reviewed by immigration counsel.
- Every religious claim verified by qualified Islamic reviewers; explain school differences.
- Detention does not automatically mean criminal conviction; religious access is never guaranteed in practice.
- Outcome branches reflect actual process constraints and uncertainty.
- No personally identifying detainee stories, graphic suffering, immigration-evasion tips or coercive gameplay.
- No runtime implementation until explicitly authorized.
