# Dunya — The Battle for a Better World (campaign world bible)
Status: proposed fictional narrative; documentation only, no gameplay logic.

## Logline
Across a fictional network of global cities, Ahmed and Fatimah discover that exploitative institutions profit from rage, dependence, secrecy and injustice. Guided by faith, knowledge and solidarity, they build ethical alternatives industry by industry. Shaytan is a theological unseen antagonist/temptation, never a physically depicted character, and no character can claim to know the unseen. Corporate antagonists are fictional composites, not coded allegations about real groups, religions, nationalities or governments.

## Tone and guardrails
Hopeful, high-stakes civic and economic drama with moral ambiguity and credible competing perspectives. No stereotyping, demonization, sectarian scapegoating, forced religious conversion, vigilante violence, hacking, sabotage, covert interference, or unfounded allegations. Protagonists protect innocent people of all faiths. The campaign's 'combat' is lawful competition, evidence, public-interest journalism, community organizing, transparency and institution building.

## Character arcs
Ahmed: volunteer -> entrepreneur -> ethical infrastructure organizer -> optional Path to Imam student. Temptations: ego, shortcuts, revenge, vanity, misplaced certainty.
Fatimah: educator -> investigative organizer -> founder of community systems -> mentor. Temptations: burnout, distrust, public attention, compromise of privacy.
NPCs include good-faith employees inside flawed institutions, whistleblowers with protections, skeptical non-Muslims, Muslim and non-Muslim partners, regulators, journalists and affected families. No character is automatically evil by profession or identity.

## Main campaign chapters
1. **The Attention Trap** — fictional ragebait app; research incentive design, interview users, build a privacy-first platform, run transparent creator migration and trust/safety.
2. **The Narrative Machine** — sensational media; evidence verification, corrections, independent local newsroom, transparent funding and press ethics.
3. **The Broken Food Chain** — unsafe/inequitable food supply; welfare, halal certification review, traceability, cooperatives, food rescue and fair pricing.
4. **The Debt Empire** — predatory financing; transparent fees, financial literacy, Sharia-reviewed alternatives, risk controls and fair access.
5. **The Watching Eye** — invasive data brokers; local inference, privacy-preserving analytics, encryption, user consent, security audit and digital rights advocacy. No offensive cyber instructions.
6. **The Supply Chain** — opaque procurement; transparent sourcing, labor rights, fair-trade networks, ethical contracts, supplier verification and lawful logistics.
7. **The Housing Trap** — predatory housing; tenant education, mediation, community land trusts and accessible construction.
8. **The Education Machine** — exploitative educational technology; open curricula, teacher empowerment, privacy and accessible learning.
9. **The Healthcare Monopoly** — exploitative access; community clinics, affordable procurement, patient privacy and evidence-based care.
10. **The Captured City** — corruption and undue influence; public records, transparent hearings, peaceful advocacy, anti-corruption safeguards and fair laws.
11. **The Great Distraction** — cross-industry systemic pressure; federate ethical alternatives, ensure sustainable governance, resist becoming a monopoly yourself.

## Standard chapter arc
A. Everyday community harm is observed without simplistic villains.
B. Investigate using consent, evidence and lawful means; distinguish fact, inference and rumor.
C. Ethical dilemma and spiritual reflection, with optional private niyyah.
D. Design an alternative with constraints: capital, labor, safety, governance, maintenance and adoption.
E. Pilot with community; solicit critical feedback and fix defects.
F. Scale sustainably, partner across faiths, measure actual social outcomes.
G. Post-launch accountability: transparency, audit, repair unintended harms.
H. A new chapter opens through a logical supply-chain, media or finance dependency.

## Metrics
Community trust, access, safety, affordability, user autonomy, fairness, resilience, transparency and sustainability. Never measure divine reward, conversions, sin counts or piety. No pay-to-win religious status.

## Source review
Research leads: Qur'an 5:2 (cooperate in good), 4:135 (justice), 49:6 (verify news), 83:1–3 (fair measure), 2:188 (unjust wealth), 16:125 (wisdom), 2:256 (no compulsion), 7:56 (avoid corruption). These are not automatically validated applications. Each story claim requires Quran.com/Sunnah.com skills, scholarly review and legal/cultural checks.

## Distinction between narrative and real-world politics
No claim that a real company or agency is directed by Shaytan or coordinated to eradicate Islam. Real industry case studies may appear only in separate, fact-checked educational commentary with fair attribution, independent verification, defamation review and user-friendly corrections.
