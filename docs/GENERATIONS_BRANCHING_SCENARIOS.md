# Generations Saga — branching scenarios and narrative dependencies
Status: proposed writing specs only; no gameplay implementation or verified religious rulings.

## Chapter A: A New Household
Ahmed or Fatimah enters a consenting adult marriage storyline or chooses an equally complete non-marriage service arc. A fictional wedding-services platform pressures expensive spending and misleading financing. Choices: transparent budget, family mediation, smaller celebration, or postpone. Consequences: trust, savings and community ties, not divine merit. Requires reviewed nikah/mahr/witness/civil-law content.

## Chapter B: A Child's First Years
A child-care advertising network promotes addictive attention products. Family choices: establish age-appropriate device boundaries, community play, parent support, accessible education. Include guardians and adoptive/care arrangements sensitively. Avoid blaming parents for disability, illness or child outcomes.

## Chapter C: The School Rumor
An algorithm spreads misinformation about a classmate. Players verify claims, apologize when wrong, support school safeguarding and build a parent-teacher information channel. No public shaming of children; Qur'an 49:6 is a source lead, not an automatic ruling.

## Chapter D: The Elder Care Contract
A fictional care agency conceals fees and neglects accessibility. Player interviews caregivers, documents concerns with consent, negotiates transparent care and seeks professional services. Older NPC retains agency and privacy; do not equate disability with incapacity.

## Chapter E: The Last Visit
A relative is seriously ill. Options: coordinate visits, listen, respect patient wishes, support family and arrange practical help. Death is neither inevitable nor a score-based failure. If a death branch occurs, player may skip grief scenes.

## Chapter F: The Janazah Committee
A family confronts funeral logistics, religious school differences and expensive upsells. Choices: seek mosque/funeral professional advice, obtain transparent quotes, arrange lawful paperwork, ensure accessible attendance, offer condolences. No graphic ritual and no time-pressure scoring on grief.

## Chapter G: The Missing Estate Documents
An administrator finds incomplete records, debts and competing heir claims. Choices: inventory, independent mediation, lawyer and scholar review, protection of minors and lawful distribution. Never let a player guess inheritance shares as authoritative law.

## Chapter H: The Next Generation
A child becomes an adult with their own independent choices, inherits community relationships and perhaps a family business or civic project only after lawful ownership transfer. Character's faith, worth and moral character are not inherited as numeric stats.

## Continuity constraints
- Death may precede marriage, occur after divorce or leave no heirs; all cases need graceful fallback.
- Guardianship is not automatically biological parentage or inheritance entitlement.
- Death and funeral do not instantly transfer titles or money.
- Minor NPC cannot consent to adult marriage; no child sexualization.
- An NPC's spouse is not their property; agency and safety remain independent.
- Story can progress without marriage, biological children, or a funeral scene.
- Jurisdiction, school of law, date and source review gate any legal/religious claim.
- No forced emotional disclosures or real-world family-data collection.

## Acceptance examples
A player declines marriage and still completes the campaign; funeral scene skipped and estate story remains comprehensible; disputed inheritance freezes allocation pending review; child safeguarding intervention avoids public exposure; no financial reward for a relative's death.
